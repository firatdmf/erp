{% trans "What happens if a difference is never recorded" %}

{% blocktrans with base=report.base_code %}Nothing goes wrong with what the customer owes: that figure is in their own currency and is always right. What drifts is the book's side of it.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Take a €1,000 sale booked when the euro was 1.10 — the book carries $1,100. The customer pays in full months later at 1.25, and that payment leaves the book at $1,250. They now owe €0, and the book is left carrying −$150 — an account that reads “We Owe” for money nobody owes anybody.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Left alone those residuals pile up: Accounts Receivable stops reconciling against what customers actually owe, and a real exchange loss sits hidden inside the receivable instead of reaching 5900, where it can be read as what it is.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Recording early costs nothing. Each new movement is booked at its own rate, and this page simply shows whatever the rate has done since.{% endblocktrans %}
